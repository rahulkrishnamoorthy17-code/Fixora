Microwave Sparking? Here's What It Means
and What To Do First

June 22, 2026 4 min read Troubleshooting

Blue-white arcs dancing inside your microwave is genuinely alarming — but in most cases it's a $15 part, not a dead appliance. The critical thing is what you do in the first sixty seconds. Here's the field guide from 2,100+ microwave repairs.

The 60-Second Safety Routine

  1. Stop the cycle immediately. Hit Stop, then unplug the unit — don't just open the door.
  2. Look for the obvious. Foil-lined packaging? A twist-tie? A fork left in leftovers? Metal inside the cavity causes arcing that looks dramatic but harms nothing once removed.
  3. If nothing metal was inside, the microwave itself has a fault. Keep it unplugged until inspected.
  4. Never run it "to see if it happens again." Repeated arcing burns the waveguide cover and can permanently damage the magnetron.

Cause 1: A Burnt Waveguide Cover (Most Common)

The waveguide cover is the small rectangular panel — usually mica sheet — on the side or top wall of the cavity. It protects the magnetron from grease splatter. When food residue carbonises on it, the burnt spots become arcing points. Replacement covers cost $8–$18 and take ten minutes to fit. This is the single most common sparking repair we perform.

Cause 2: Damaged Cavity Paint

Chipped or burned paint inside the cavity exposes bare metal, which arcs under microwave energy. Small blemishes can be safely refinished with microwave-safe cavity paint; extensive burning near the waveguide opening warrants professional assessment.

Cause 3: Diode or Magnetron Trouble

If sparking comes with humming, weak heating or a burning smell, the high-voltage section may be failing. A degraded HV diode or a struggling magnetron can cause internal arcing. This one's strictly professional territory — capacitors inside store lethal charge even when unplugged.

The dangerous cause: repeated unexplained arcing with a burning smell can precede fire. If you've had two sparking episodes with no metal involved, retire the unit until repaired.

What It Is NOT

  • Not radiation leaking out. Arcing happens inside the cavity; the door shield contains microwaves regardless. Still, have leakage tested after any repair — we do this as standard.
  • Not automatically "buy a new one." Three of the four causes above cost less than $80 to fix properly.
  • Not safe to ignore. Every spark scars the cavity a little more, and scarring begets more sparks.

Prevention: Two Habits

Wipe the cavity weekly — especially around the waveguide cover — and never microwave anything metallic, including "microwave-safe-ish" foil-edged containers and vintage plates with gold trim. Those two habits prevent about 95% of the sparking calls we see.
